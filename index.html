Overview

Robot learning has reached a body-selection problem. Open X-Embodiment pooled data from 22 robot embodiments and showed positive cross-robot transfer with RT-X policies, and generalist policies such as π0 and GR00T N1 now extend cross-embodiment training to manipulation and humanoid platforms. This progress does not remove embodiment choice. It raises the stakes: if policies can transfer across platforms, researchers must decide whether to adapt the policy to an existing robot, modify the hardware, or build a task-specific body.

In this workshop, specialists are task-specific robot bodies and generalists are policies and platforms intended to work across many bodies and tasks. The central CoRL question is when a specialist body is justified in a field increasingly shaped by generalist robot learning.

Today that decision lacks agreed evidence standards. Learned evaluators, differentiable simulation, grammar-based search, generative design, and text-conditioned tools may screen large spaces of candidate bodies before fabrication. But a learned ranking is not yet a design decision. A mechanics calculation, controller-training run, optimization baseline, or a single hardware test can overturn it.

Unlike broad co-design, embodiment-aware learning, or morphology-aware design-learning workshops, this workshop focuses on the evidence chain for design decisions: ranking candidate bodies, validating those rankings, and deciding when a body is worth building. Its output will be a practical validation artifact: a learned-evaluator trustworthiness checklist, benchmark desiderata, and reporting guidance for robot-body selection claims.

  • Specialist-generalist decision boundary: when should a team adapt a policy to an existing platform, modify the hardware, or build a task-specific body?
  • Trustworthy learned evaluators: when do learned embodiment rankings survive controller retraining, sim-to-real transfer, and hardware tests?
  • Validation before fabrication: what evidence is needed before committing to expensive prototypes?
  • Hybrid co-design pipelines: how should learned models, optimization, control theory, and hardware tests interact?
  • Benchmarks and metrics: what should credible co-design benchmarks measure before fabrication?

Selected Background

  • Open X-Embodiment: cross-robot datasets and RT-X policies.
  • Hardware as Policy and Fit2Form: learning-based hardware and gripper design.
  • ChainQueen, RoboGrammar, and Text2Robot: simulation, grammar, and generative tools for exploring body designs.
  • ICRA 2024 Co-design in Robotics, RSS 2024 EARL, CoRL 2024 MAPoDeL, RSS 2025 Brain and Brawn, and CoRL 2025 Generalist Policies in the Wild: adjacent workshops that motivate this narrower evidence-standards focus.

Call for Contributions

We will invite 2-4 page extended abstracts on robotic co-design, with particular interest in work that connects embodiment choices to large-scale learning. Technical abstracts, position pieces, benchmark proposals, work in progress, and negative results are welcome. Papers already accepted to the main CoRL 2026 conference will be discouraged. Accepted contributions will appear as posters, with selected abstracts giving short pitches before the poster session. Their questions will feed the working-group sessions.

Relevant topics include learned embodiment evaluators, morphology-aware policy learning, differentiable simulation for design, hardware-control co-optimization, real-to-sim and sim-to-real validation for morphology, foundation models for robot design, and benchmarks for embodiment selection. Abstracts will receive at least two single-blind reviews for relevance to the workshop challenges, technical clarity, novelty, and potential to seed discussion. Reviewers with conflicts will recuse themselves.

We will circulate the call through robotics-worldwide, Women in Robotics, Black in AI, LatinX in AI, soft robotics and mechanisms-and-design lists, embodied-AI channels, and regional robotics networks represented by the organizers. A portion of poster-pitch slots and working-group roles will be reserved for student-led and early-career contributions.

Invited speakers will stay involved beyond their talks by seeding working-group worksheets, joining the report-back or fishbowl discussion, and helping identify artifact leads.

Submission details, reviewing criteria, and deadlines will be posted after workshop acceptance. Questions: kevinxqiu@gmail.com.

Expected Output

Working-group worksheets will seed a 4-8 page CoRL-format community report or position paper titled Choosing Robot Bodies: Specialists and Generalists in Robot Co-Design, on evidence standards for learning-based robot co-design. The artifact will turn workshop discussion into reusable guidance rather than a transcript of talks. Organizers will appoint artifact leads during the closing session, circulate an outline within two weeks, and submit the public report within four weeks after the conference.

  • Trustworthiness checklist: when learned embodiment rankings are credible enough to influence design search.
  • Benchmark desiderata: tasks, metrics, and reporting standards for comparing candidate bodies.
  • Validation protocols: what simulation, mechanics, policy training, and hardware evidence should be reported before claiming that a body is worth building.
